# Outreach Guidelines

> This file defines the tone, structure, and standards for all outreach drafted by Claude. The `/draft-outreach` command reads this to produce consistent, high-quality emails and LinkedIn messages.

---

## Tone & Voice

**Highly professional, confident, and direct.**

- Write like a senior leader reaching out peer-to-peer, not a salesperson begging for time
- Be specific about why you're reaching out. Every message must have a clear, relevant reason tied to something the brand is doing
- Always include a **clear Call to Action**, typically a request for a short meeting or call
- Convey credibility through relevance and insight, not through listing credentials or awards
- Keep it tight. Respect the reader's time

### Voice principles

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Lead with what you noticed about their brand | Lead with who hrmny is |
| Be specific and sharp | Be vague or generic |
| Sound like a peer, not a vendor | Sound salesy or desperate |
| One clear CTA | Multiple asks or soft endings |
| Reference a real signal (launch, campaign, hire, gap) | Use filler like "I'd love to explore synergies" |
| Keep sentences short and confident | Write long, winding paragraphs |

## Cold Email Structure

**Target length**: 150-200 words. Subject line + body.

### Subject line principles
- Short (5-8 words)
- Specific to the brand or their situation
- No clickbait, no ALL CAPS, no emojis
- Should read like something a peer would write, not a mass email
- Examples: "Quick thought on [Brand]'s UAE rollout", "[Brand] x culture-led creative"

### Body structure

1. **Opening line** (1 sentence): Reference something specific about the brand: a recent move, campaign, market entry, or gap you noticed. This proves you've done your homework.

2. **The bridge** (1-2 sentences): Connect what they're doing to what hrmny does. Frame it as an observation or opportunity, not a pitch.

3. **Credibility signal** (1 sentence, optional): One line that builds trust: a relevant client category, a similar challenge you've solved, or a regional insight. Not a credential dump.

4. **CTA** (1 sentence): Direct ask for a meeting. Specific timeframe. Make it easy to say yes.
   - "Would you be open to a 20-minute call this week?"
   - "Happy to share how we approached this for a similar brand. Worth a quick call?"

5. **Sign-off**: Keep it simple and direct.

### Email template skeleton

```
Subject: [Specific to brand/situation]

Hi [First name],

[Opening: specific observation about their brand/company]

[Bridge: connection to hrmny's capability, framed as an opportunity]

[Optional credibility: one relevant proof point]

[CTA: direct ask for a meeting with a specific timeframe]

Best,
Ayham
```

## LinkedIn Connection Request

**Hard limit: 300 characters.** Must be punchy, personalised, and professional.

### Structure
- 1 sentence: Why you're connecting (specific to them)
- 1 sentence: What you want to discuss or why it's relevant
- No hard CTA in the connection request itself; save that for the follow-up

### Principles
- Reference their role, company, or something they've posted/done
- Don't pitch in the connection request. It's an introduction
- Sound like a human, not a template
- Professional but not stiff

### Example formats

```
Hi [Name], noticed [Brand]'s push into the UAE market. We work with global brands
on culture-led creative in the region. Would be great to connect.
```

```
Hi [Name], saw [Brand]'s recent [campaign/launch/move]. We specialise in exactly
this space across the GCC. Would love to be in your network.
```

## LinkedIn Follow-Up Message

**Sent after connection is accepted.** ~100 words. This is where the CTA lives.

### Structure

1. **Thank + context** (1 sentence): Thanks for connecting, reference why you reached out
2. **Value line** (1-2 sentences): What hrmny does that's relevant to their brand, framed as useful to them
3. **CTA** (1 sentence): Ask for a call or meeting. Specific and easy

### Example

```
Thanks for connecting, [Name].

I reached out because [specific reason: their brand activity, market move, etc.].
We work with brands like [relevant category example] on [relevant service] across
the UAE and GCC, and I think there's a strong fit with what [Brand] is building.

Would you be open to a quick 15-minute call this week? Happy to share a few ideas.

Best,
Ayham
```

## Personalisation Requirements

**Every outreach must include at least one of these**:

- A specific reference to the company's recent activity (launch, campaign, hire, expansion)
- An observation about their current marketing/creative (social presence, PR coverage, brand positioning)
- A relevant market or category insight that affects their business
- A reference to a mutual connection or shared context

**Never send generic outreach.** If you can't find a personalisation angle, research harder or flag it.

---

## What to Avoid

- "I'd love to explore synergies" or any vague language
- "We're a full-service agency" or similar positioning that says nothing
- Leading with hrmny's history, team size, or awards
- Multiple CTAs in one message
- Overly casual tone (no "Hey!", no emojis, no slang)
- Long paragraphs or walls of text
- Mentioning pricing in cold outreach
- Apologising for reaching out ("Sorry to bother you...")
- **Em dashes** in any outreach writing. Use commas, periods, colons, or semicolons instead
