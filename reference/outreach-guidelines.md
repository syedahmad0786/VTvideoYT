## Tone & Voice

**Peer-to-peer. Confident. Specific. Brief.**

Write like a Managing Partner reaching out to a fellow senior leader because something they are doing caught your attention. Never write like a vendor pitching services.

### Voice Principles

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Lead with what you noticed about their brand | Lead with who hrmny is |
| Be specific and sharp | Be vague or generic |
| Sound like a peer, not a vendor | Sound salesy or desperate |
| One clear CTA per message | Multiple asks or soft endings |
| Reference a real signal (launch, campaign, hire, gap) | Use filler like "I'd love to explore synergies" |
| Keep sentences short and confident | Write long, winding paragraphs |
| Name the specific opportunity you see | Describe hrmny's full service menu |
| Match tone to the contact's seniority and sector | Use the same tone for a CMO and a Brand Manager |

## Multi-Touch Cadence

Every lead gets a structured sequence. The cadence depends on whether we have a verified email.

### Full cadence (email + LinkedIn)

Used when `/fetch-contacts` returns a verified email address.

| Touch | Channel | Timing | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Email** | Day 0 | Primary cold outreach. Specific hook + CTA |
| 2 | **LinkedIn connection request** | Day 1 | Reinforce. Reference the email only if natural |
| 3 | **LinkedIn follow-up message** | 24-48 hours after connection accepted | Value-forward message with CTA |
| 4 | **Email follow-up** | Day 5 (if no reply to Touch 1) | New angle or additional value. Not "just checking in" |
| 5 | **LinkedIn nudge** | Day 10 (if no reply anywhere) | Light re-engagement. Share a relevant insight |
| 6 | **Break-up email** | Day 18 (if no reply anywhere) | Final touch. Low-pressure, leaves door open |

### LinkedIn-only cadence (no email)

Used when no verified email is available.

| Touch | Channel | Timing | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **LinkedIn connection request** | Day 0 | Personalised, specific to their brand |
| 2 | **LinkedIn follow-up message** | 24-48 hours after connection accepted | Full pitch with CTA |
| 3 | **LinkedIn value-add** | Day 7 (if no reply) | Share a relevant insight, article, or observation |
| 4 | **LinkedIn break-up** | Day 16 (if no reply) | Final touch. Light, respectful close |

### Cadence rules

- **Never send Touch 1 email and Touch 2 LinkedIn on the same day.** Minimum 24-hour gap.
- **LinkedIn follow-up (Touch 3) timing is fixed**: 24-48 hours after they accept the connection. Not "whenever." If they accept on Day 8, send the follow-up on Day 9-10.
- **Each follow-up must add new value.** A different angle, a new insight, a relevant example. Never repeat the same message or just "bump" the thread.
- **Break-up messages are mandatory.** They often get the highest reply rates. Keep them short, low-pressure, and final.
- **If they reply at any point**, stop the cadence and shift to conversation mode.

## Cold Email Structure

**Target length**: 150-200 words. Subject line + body. Every word earns its place.

### Power Opening Framework

The opening line is the single highest-leverage sentence in the email. It must pass this test: **Could this sentence only be written about this specific company?** If you could swap in another brand name and the sentence still works, it is too generic. Rewrite it.

**Formula**: [Specific thing you observed] + [why it caught your attention]

**Strong openings** (passes the specificity test):

- "Saw that Lucid just opened its first UAE showroom on Sheikh Zayed Road. The positioning as a lifestyle brand, not just an EV, is a different play from what the other new entrants are doing here."
- "Your Ramadan campaign with the calligraphy-forward packaging got picked up across Arabic social. That kind of cultural fluency is rare from a global FMCG brand in the region."
- "Noticed On Running just signed a lease in Dubai Mall and is hiring a regional marketing lead. That signals a big push, not a toe-dip."

**Weak openings** (fails the specificity test):

- "I came across your brand and was impressed by your work." (Could be anyone.)
- "Your company is doing interesting things in the UAE market." (Says nothing.)
- "As a leader in the [industry] space, you know how important marketing is." (Generic flattery.)

### Body Structure

1. **Power opening** (1-2 sentences): The specific observation. Proves you did the work. Sets the frame for the entire email.

2. **The bridge** (1-2 sentences): Connect their situation to a specific opportunity. Frame it as something they should be thinking about, not something you want to sell.
   - Good: "With the Dubai showroom live, the question becomes how to make the brand feel local without losing the global edge. That's a specific challenge we've cracked for brands entering this market."
   - Bad: "We offer social media management, PR, and branding services."

3. **Credibility signal** (1 sentence, optional): One proof point. A category reference, not a client name drop. Keep it oblique.
   - Good: "We've done this for another premium automotive brand entering the GCC."
   - Bad: "We've worked with 50+ brands and have 33 employees."

4. **CTA** (1 sentence): Direct. Specific. Easy to say yes to.
   - "Open to a 20-minute call next week?"
   - "Worth a quick conversation? I can share how we approached this for a similar brand."
   - "Can I send over one idea we had for [Brand]'s UAE rollout?"

5. **Sign-off**: "Best, Ayham" or "Ayham". Nothing more.

### Email Template Skeleton

```
Subject: [5-8 words, specific to their brand/situation]

Hi [First name],

[Power opening: specific observation that could only apply to this brand]

[Bridge: connect their situation to a specific opportunity]

[Optional credibility: one oblique proof point from a similar category]

[CTA: direct ask, specific timeframe, easy to accept]

Best,
Ayham
```

## Subject Line Playbook

**Rules**: 5-8 words. Specific to the brand or situation. No clickbait, no ALL CAPS, no emojis, no punctuation tricks. Should look like a peer wrote it, not a marketing automation tool.

### Proven Patterns

**Pattern 1: [Brand] + situation**
- "Lucid's UAE launch + creative gap"
- "On Running's Dubai push"
- "Samsung Gulf's Ramadan window"

**Pattern 2: Quick thought on [topic]**
- "Quick thought on [Brand]'s GCC rollout"
- "Quick thought on your retail launch"
- "Idea for [Brand]'s UAE presence"

**Pattern 3: [Brand] x [concept]**
- "[Brand] x culture-led creative"
- "[Brand] x regional storytelling"
- "[Brand] x community in the UAE"

**Pattern 4: Observation-led**
- "Noticed your Dubai Mall opening"
- "Saw [Brand]'s regional hiring push"
- "Your Ramadan campaign caught my eye"

**Pattern 5: Question format**
- "Who's running [Brand]'s UAE creative?"
- "Is [Brand] investing in GCC content?"

**Pattern 6: Re-engagement / follow-up**
- "One more thought on [Brand]"
- "Closing the loop on [Brand]'s UAE push"
- "Worth revisiting, [First name]?"

### Subject Line Selection

- **Touch 1** (cold email): Use Pattern 1, 2, or 4. Anchor to the specific signal.
- **Touch 4** (follow-up email): Use Pattern 6 or a fresh Pattern 3. Must feel different from Touch 1.
- **Touch 6** (break-up): Use Pattern 6. Keep it soft and final.
- **A/B testing**: When drafting in batch mode, alternate between two patterns for the same sector to compare reply rates. See [A/B Testing Protocol](#ab-testing-protocol).

## LinkedIn Connection Request

**Hard limit: 300 characters.** Punchy, personalised, professional. This is an introduction, not a pitch.

### Structure

- 1 sentence: Why you are connecting (specific to them or their brand)
- 1 sentence: What makes it relevant or what you want to discuss
- No hard CTA. Save the ask for the follow-up message

### Principles

- Reference their role, company, or something they have posted or done
- Do not pitch services in the connection request
- Sound human. Not templated
- Professional but not stiff. Match the platform's tone
- If an email was sent (Touch 1), do not repeat the same hook verbatim. Use a complementary angle

### Examples

```
Hi [Name], noticed [Brand]'s push into the UAE market. We work with global brands
on culture-led creative in the region. Would be great to connect.
```

```
Hi [Name], saw [Brand]'s recent [campaign/launch/move]. We specialise in exactly
this space across the GCC. Would love to be in your network.
```

```
Hi [Name], your team's work on [specific campaign] stood out. I lead growth at a
Dubai creative agency in the same space. Would be good to connect.
```

```
Hi [Name], [Brand]'s regional expansion caught my attention. We've helped similar
brands land their creative in the UAE. Happy to connect.
```

## LinkedIn Follow-Up Message

**Sent 24-48 hours after connection is accepted.** Not "sometime after." Not "when you get to it." 24-48 hours. This is the conversion moment: they just accepted, you are top of mind.

**Target length**: ~100 words. This is where the CTA lives.

### Structure

1. **Thank + context** (1 sentence): Thanks for connecting. Brief reference to why you reached out. Do not repeat the connection request verbatim.
2. **Value line** (1-2 sentences): What hrmny does that is specifically relevant to their brand. Frame as useful to them, not a service list.
3. **CTA** (1 sentence): Direct ask for a call. Specific timeframe. Easy to say yes.

### Template

```
Thanks for connecting, [Name].

I reached out because [specific reason tied to their brand activity or market move].
We work with brands like [relevant category, not a name drop] on [specific service]
across the UAE, and I think there's a strong fit with what [Brand] is building here.

Would you be open to a 15-minute call this week? Happy to share a couple of ideas
specific to [Brand].

Best,
Ayham
```

### Follow-Up Rules

- **Timing is non-negotiable**: 24-48 hours after acceptance. Set it and execute.
- If they accepted but did not reply to the follow-up after 5 days, send a **value-add message** (share a relevant article, insight, or observation about their brand). Do not re-pitch.
- If they never accept the connection request after 14 days, do not resend. Mark as "No Response" and move on.

## A/B Testing Protocol

Testing is how outreach gets better. Every batch run is a testing opportunity.

### What to Test

| Element | How to Test | Minimum Sample |
|---|---|---|
| **Subject lines** | Alternate 2 patterns across same-sector leads in a batch | 10 emails per variant |
| **Opening hooks** | Test observation-led vs. question-led openings | 10 emails per variant |
| **CTA phrasing** | "Open to a call?" vs. "Can I send you one idea?" | 10 emails per variant |
| **LinkedIn connection note** | Short (1 sentence) vs. full (2 sentences) | 10 requests per variant |

### How to Track

- When drafting in batch mode (`/draft-outreach` without arguments), assign **Variant A** to the first half and **Variant B** to the second half
- Tag each outreach row in Google Sheets with the variant (A or B) in the Notes column
- After 14 days, compare reply rates between variants
- `/reflect` should surface variant performance when analysing outreach outcomes

### Decision Rules

- A variant "wins" when it has **2x or more** difference in reply rate with at least 10 sends per variant
- Winning patterns get added to this file via `/evolve`
- Losing patterns get noted in `memory/outreach-learnings.md` and retired
- If no clear winner after 20+ sends per variant, the test is inconclusive. Move on to the next variable

## Sector-Specific Hooks

Use these as starting frameworks. Every hook must still be personalised to the specific company.

### Retail + Consumer Experience

**Signals to look for**: new store openings, flagship launches, F&B expansion, seasonal campaigns (DSF, Ramadan, summer), experiential retail investments, loyalty programme launches, regional e-commerce push.

**Hook angles**:
- "Dubai retail is in an experience arms race. The brands winning aren't just opening stores, they're building moments. [Specific observation about what this brand is doing or missing]."
- "Your [store/concept] launch in [location] is well-timed. The question most brands miss at this stage is how to turn foot traffic into a content engine that compounds."
- "Noticed [Brand] is expanding to [X] locations in the UAE this year. That's a scale play, but the creative usually lags behind the real estate. Has that been on your radar?"

**Credibility angles**: experience-led retail campaigns, social content systems for multi-location brands, F&B launch activations.

### Sports / Wellness / Movements

**Signals to look for**: new market entry, athlete/ambassador signings, community-building programmes, event sponsorships, studio/gym openings, performance-lifestyle positioning, wellness brand launches.

**Hook angles**:
- "The sports and wellness space in the UAE is exploding, but most brands are still running generic fitness influencer plays. [Brand] seems to be building something with more depth. [Specific observation]."
- "Community-first brands are winning in this region. [Brand]'s [specific programme/event/initiative] has the right DNA for it. The gap is usually in how that translates to always-on content and earned media."
- "Saw [Brand] is sponsoring [event/athlete] in the UAE. That's a smart entry point. The brands that get the most from these deals are the ones who build a content and PR layer around them, not just logo placement."

**Credibility angles**: community-driven brand campaigns, movement marketing, sports culture content, event activation.

### Automotive (incl. EV entrants)

**Signals to look for**: showroom openings, new model launches in GCC, regional marketing hires, lifestyle brand positioning (especially EV), motorsport sponsorships, sustainability messaging.

**Hook angles**:
- "The new EV entrants in the GCC are fighting for the same audience, but most are defaulting to specs-and-range messaging. [Brand]'s positioning as a [lifestyle/luxury/performance] brand is the right play. The question is how to make that land culturally in the UAE."
- "Noticed [Brand] just opened in [location]. The UAE automotive buyer is uniquely brand-driven. First impressions in this market set the trajectory."
- "Your [model] launch is getting coverage, but the earned media angle feels underplayed. There's an opportunity to turn a product launch into a cultural moment."

**Credibility angles**: automotive brand launches in GCC, lifestyle-positioned automotive campaigns, experiential showroom activations.

## Personalisation Requirements

**Every outreach must include at least one of these. Non-negotiable.**

- A specific reference to the company's recent activity (launch, campaign, hire, expansion, funding)
- An observation about their current marketing or creative presence (social quality, PR gaps, brand positioning)
- A relevant market or category insight that directly affects their business
- A reference to a mutual connection or shared context

**The specificity test**: If you can swap in another company's name and the message still reads naturally, it is not personalised enough. Rewrite.

**If you cannot find a personalisation angle** after research, flag it to Ayham. Do not send generic outreach.

## No-Response & Objection Handling

### Re-engagement Templates (for no-response after full cadence)

**Email follow-up (Touch 4, Day 5)**: New angle, not a reminder.

```
Subject: One more thought on [Brand]'s UAE push

Hi [First name],

Since I last reached out, I noticed [new development: a campaign they ran, a hire
they made, a competitor move]. It reinforced the thought that [specific opportunity].

[One sentence connecting to hrmny's relevance]

Still worth a quick conversation? Happy to keep it to 15 minutes.

Best,
Ayham
```

**Break-up email (Touch 6, Day 18)**: Low-pressure close.

```
Subject: Closing the loop, [First name]

Hi [First name],

I've reached out a couple of times about [specific topic]. I know timing isn't
always right, so I'll leave it here.

If [Brand] ever needs a creative partner who understands the UAE market at a
cultural level, I'm an easy person to reach.

Best,
Ayham
```

**LinkedIn value-add (Touch 5, Day 10)**: Share, don't sell.

```
[Name], came across [relevant article / industry data point / competitor example]
and thought of [Brand]'s position in the UAE. Figured it might be useful.

[Link or 1-sentence insight]

No agenda, just thought it was relevant to what you're building.
```

**LinkedIn break-up (Touch 4 in LinkedIn-only cadence, Day 16)**:

```
[Name], I'll keep it brief. If [Brand] ever needs a creative partner in the UAE
who gets the culture side, happy to chat. No pressure.
```

### Handling Common Objections

**"We already have an agency."**
- "Totally understand. Most brands we work with started as a second opinion on a specific project. If there's ever a brief that needs a fresh perspective, I'd welcome the chance."

**"Not the right time."**
- "Appreciate the honesty. When would be a better window? Happy to reconnect in [Q2 / after Ramadan / in 3 months]."
- Log the follow-up date in the sheet. Set a reminder.

**"Send me some info / a deck."**
- Do not treat this as a win. It is often a polite deflection.
- Send a short, tailored one-pager (not a generic capabilities deck). Include 2-3 relevant examples, not a portfolio dump.
- Follow up 3 days later with a specific question tied to their brand, not "Did you get the deck?"

**"We handle everything in-house."**
- "Makes sense. A lot of the brands we work with have strong in-house teams and bring us in for specific lifts: a campaign, a launch, Ramadan activation. If that ever comes up, I'd be a good call."

**"What are your rates?"**
- Never quote rates in cold outreach. Redirect to a conversation.
- "It depends on scope. Typically we work on retainers starting around AED 20-25K/month, but the best next step is a 15-minute call to see if there's even a fit first."

## Response Rate Benchmarks

Use these to measure outreach performance. Targets are for hrmny's profile (agency, B2B, senior decision-makers in the UAE).

### Email Benchmarks

| Metric | Poor | Acceptable | Good | Excellent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Open rate** | <30% | 30-45% | 45-60% | >60% |
| **Reply rate** (all replies) | <3% | 3-5% | 5-10% | >10% |
| **Positive reply rate** (interested) | <1% | 1-3% | 3-5% | >5% |
| **Bounce rate** | >5% | 3-5% | 1-3% | <1% |
| **Meeting booked rate** | <1% | 1-2% | 2-4% | >4% |

### LinkedIn Benchmarks

| Metric | Poor | Acceptable | Good | Excellent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Connection acceptance rate** | <20% | 20-35% | 35-50% | >50% |
| **Reply rate** (after follow-up) | <5% | 5-10% | 10-20% | >20% |
| **Positive reply rate** | <2% | 2-5% | 5-10% | >10% |
| **Meeting booked rate** | <1% | 1-3% | 3-5% | >5% |

### Cadence Benchmarks

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Avg. touches before first reply | 2-3 |
| Touch that generates most replies | Touch 4 (follow-up email) or Touch 6 (break-up) |
| Optimal send day (UAE) | Sunday-Wednesday |
| Optimal send time (UAE) | 8:00-10:00 AM GST or 2:00-3:00 PM GST |
| Break-up email reply rate vs. cold email | Typically 1.5-2x higher |

### Performance Review Triggers

- If reply rate drops below **3%** across 30+ sends, escalate to `/reflect` and review hooks, subject lines, and ICP targeting
- If LinkedIn acceptance rate drops below **20%**, review connection request copy and targeting seniority
- If meeting-booked rate stays below **1%** for 2+ weeks, review CTA strength and follow-up discipline
- `/reflect` should compare against these benchmarks automatically when analysing outreach data

## What to Avoid

- "I'd love to explore synergies" or any vague language
- "We're a full-service agency" or similar positioning that says nothing
- Leading with hrmny's history, team size, or awards
- Multiple CTAs in one message
- Overly casual tone (no "Hey!", no emojis, no slang)
- Long paragraphs or walls of text
- Mentioning pricing in cold outreach (redirect to a conversation)
- Apologising for reaching out ("Sorry to bother you...")
- **Em dashes** in any outreach writing. Use commas, periods, colons, or semicolons instead
- "Just checking in" or "Just following up" as an opening line. Every follow-up must add new value
- Sending the same hook across email and LinkedIn. Use complementary angles
- Generic capability decks when someone asks for info. Send a tailored one-pager
- Skipping the break-up message. It often gets the highest reply rate in the cadence
